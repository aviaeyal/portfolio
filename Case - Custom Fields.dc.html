Custom Fields

How do you let every business track the details unique to their work, without bloating the product for everyone else?

CONTEXT

BEFORE: every field fixed and locked (project name, type, date, about) except one generic short-text slot, with no way to add or type anything else.

No two service businesses track the same things. A photographer cares about shoot locations; a coach about session counts. The fixed fields forced everyone into notes and workarounds. It showed up directly in what people were asking for:

“Hi there, I'm curious if I'm able to add fields into a contact detail section. Is this possible?”

“I was able to create a new field within the project… but I'm only able to create text fields. Is it possible to create dropdowns or other types of fields?”

The challenge wasn't adding fields: it was keeping the product simple for the people who would never need them.

PROCESS

I ran discovery calls with two power users to pressure-test the model. Their expectations converged on the same handful of behaviors: renaming a field should update it everywhere it's used, not break silently the way it did in tools they'd left behind; most fields would live on the contact rather than the project; and quantity mattered less than reliability: one user's clients needed five fields or fewer 90% of the time, while the other wanted headroom up to 25.

Adding a field meant a small, focused decision: name it, then pick a type scoped to what the data actually needs, not a blank spreadsheet cell.

Text, long text, date, number, link: five types, chosen after the competitive scan showed most tools over-offer here.

To pressure-test that the five types were enough, I ran the model against a real job, a member who sends a birthday card and wants the right details ready when they do:

I also benchmarked field flexibility across seven competitor tools (Zoho, Spotler CRM, Bigin, Bloom, Copilot, Kartra, and Dubsado) to calibrate how far to push customization versus how simple to keep the defaults.
