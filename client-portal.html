04

Client Portal Redesign

HoneyBook · Client experience · 2022

How do you make a portal feel professional and trustworthy to people who may never sign in again?

Role
Product designer
Team
PM · engineering · research
Timeline
2022
Scope
Research to ship

Context

Research into what members felt HoneyBook wasn't delivering kept surfacing the same theme: customization and personalization ranked among the lowest-scoring aspects of the product, well behind usability and reliability. The old client portal was a symptom of that gap. It was a single flat page with no branding, no navigation, and no way for a member to make it feel like their own business.

And the portal's audience is unforgiving. A client may open it once, on their phone, with no idea what HoneyBook even is. Every visual cue either builds trust in the business that sent it, or undermines it.

  • 444votes for a better client portal
  • 16%of members rank client experience their #1 priority
  • Lowestwhere customization scored, of all expectation areas

Process

I designed for the dual-sided nature of the surface: white-label enough to feel like the member's brand, accessible enough to work for anyone, and pressure-tested around the first-open experience. The before and after shows the shift, from one flat unbranded page to a full workspace with navigation, project details, and files, wearing the member's own colors and imagery.

Outcome

Shipped a redesigned client portal with navigation, richer project context, and full branding control, giving members a portal that looks like their business rather than a piece of software their clients have never heard of. It became the clearest expression of the broader customization-and-control opportunity the research surfaced.
