HONEYBOOK · CX · 2025

Client Portal

How do you make a portal feel professional and trustworthy to people who may never sign in again?

The redesigned portal, with the customization panel members use to theme it: sidebar navigation, project details, messages, tasks, and files, all carrying the member's own brand.

ROLEProduct designer
TEAMPM · engineering · research
TIMELINE2025
SCOPEResearch → ship

CONTEXT

Research into what members felt HoneyBook wasn't delivering on kept surfacing the same theme: after cost, client experience was the single biggest driver of negative NPS. The old client portal was a symptom of that gap: a single flat page with no branding, no navigation, and no way for a member to make it feel like their own business, and it showed in the numbers, only 6–10% of clients ever logged into it.

And the portal's audience is unforgiving: a client may open it once, on their phone, with no idea what HoneyBook even is. Every visual cue either builds trust in the business that sent it, or undermines it.

PROCESS

We ran an unmoderated concept test with 16 active members before building anything. Side navigation beat the existing tabs decisively, 14 of 16 preferred it. For surfacing outstanding items, a notifications center beat a dedicated page or overview section once compared side by side (56% vs. 25% and 19%), and overdue invoices, unread messages, and unsigned contracts each cleared 75%+ endorsement as what belonged there. Branding tested even more decisively: 14 of 16 rated it very important, and every proposed customization option cleared a 75% essential threshold. The before/after shows the shift those findings drove: from one flat, unbranded page to a full workspace with navigation, project details, and files, wearing the member's own colors and imagery.

OUTCOME

Shipped a rebranded portal with side navigation, an outstanding-items overview, and full member-set branding, aimed squarely at the problem that started it: a portal so generic and hard to reach that only 6–10% of clients ever logged in. Once it looked like their own business, members put it in front of clients far more, and the share of clients logging in has climbed to 22.6% over the last 30 days. Both portal sharing and take-up of the new branding controls ran well past their targets.
