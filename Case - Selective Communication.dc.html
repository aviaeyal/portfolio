HONEYBOOK · COMMS · 2024

Selective Communication

How do you give a business owner control over exactly who hears from them — without turning messaging into a chore?

The Share flow — recipient selection, file authentication, and per-participant access in one place.

ROLELead product designer
TEAMPM · engineering · research
TIMELINE2024
CONTRIB.Discovery → ship

CONTEXT

A HoneyBook project is rarely one-to-one. It holds clients, collaborators, and team members — but every email and shared file went to all of them at once. One in five emails already goes to multiple participants, and 43% of members run projects with more than one client, so the wrong person seeing the wrong message was a routine, real risk.

Selective communication was the third most-requested feature in the community (146 votes). The need wasn't simply “send fewer emails” — it was control and confidence about who sees what, before hitting send.

PROCESS

I ran a study with 30 members to pressure-test the model. 61% wanted to decide case-by-case whether all past communication or only new communication is shared; 32% were uncomfortable with “share everything” as the only option. Most tellingly, 71% framed the value as not spamming clients and protecting sensitive information — not just tidiness. That reframed the design around recipient control and clear visibility, mapped against the underlying participant states.

OUTCOME

Shipped a Share flow that lets members choose recipients per email and manage file access in one place — with authentication for sensitive files and per-participant visibility across the workspace. In usability testing, all four core tasks — email a proposal, share via link, set file access, and remove a participant — tested above an 80% success rate.
