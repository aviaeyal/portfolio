01

Selective Communication

HoneyBook · Communications · 2024

How do you give a business owner control over exactly who hears from them, without turning messaging into a chore?

Role
Lead product designer
Team
PM · engineering · research
Timeline
2024
Scope
Discovery to ship

Context

A HoneyBook project is rarely one to one. It holds clients, collaborators, and team members, but every email and shared file went to all of them at once. One in five emails already goes to multiple participants, and 43% of members run two or more projects with more than one client. The wrong person seeing the wrong message was a routine, real risk.

Selective communication was the third most requested feature in the community. The need wasn't simply to send fewer emails. It was control, and confidence about who sees what, before hitting send.

  • #3most-requested community feature, 146 votes
  • 20%of emails go to more than one participant
  • 43%of members run 2+ projects with more than one client

Process

I ran a study with 30 members to pressure-test the model. 61% wanted to decide case by case whether all past communication or only new communication gets shared, and 32% were uncomfortable with share-everything as the only option. Most telling of all: 71% framed the value as not spamming clients and protecting sensitive information, not as tidiness. That reframed the design around recipient control and clear visibility, mapped against the underlying participant states.

Finding

Outcome

Shipped a Share flow that lets members choose recipients per email and manage file access in one place, with authentication for sensitive files and per-participant visibility across the workspace. In usability testing, all four core tasks (email a proposal, share via a link, set file access, remove a participant) tested above an 80% success rate.

  • 80%+success rate on every tested task
  • 4 / 4core tasks above the bar
  • 30members in the research
