Client Portal Redesign

How do you make a portal feel professional and trustworthy to people who may never sign in again?

The redesigned portal — sidebar navigation, project details, messages, tasks, and files, themed to the member's own brand.

ROLEProduct designer
TEAMPM · engineering · research
TIMELINE2022
CONTRIB.Research → ship

CONTEXT

Research into what members felt HoneyBook wasn't delivering on kept surfacing the same theme: customization and personalization ranked among the lowest-scoring aspects of the product, well behind usability and reliability. The old client portal was a symptom of that gap — a single flat page with no branding, no navigation, and no way for a member to make it feel like their own business.

And the portal's audience is unforgiving: a client may open it once, on their phone, with no idea what HoneyBook even is — every visual cue either builds trust in the business that sent it, or undermines it.

PROCESS

I designed for the dual-sided nature of the surface — white-label enough to feel like the member's brand, accessible enough to work for anyone — and pressure-tested the first-open experience. The before/after shows the shift: from one flat, unbranded page to a full workspace with navigation, project details, and files, wearing the member's own colors and imagery.

BEFORE

AFTER

The branding controls behind it — page and sidebar color, button styling, and a live preview — so the theming in the after shot is something any member can set themselves.

OUTCOME

Shipped a redesigned client portal with navigation, richer project context, and full branding control — giving members a portal that looks like their business, not a piece of software they've never heard of. It became the clearest expression of the broader "customization and control" opportunity the research surfaced.
